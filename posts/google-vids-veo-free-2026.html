What Is Google Vids (and What Is Veo 3.1)?

Google Vids is Google's browser-based video creation tool, part of Google Workspace. On April 2, 2026, Google announced that Veo 3.1 — the latest version of its AI video generation model — would be available free to all personal Google accounts through Google Vids.

This matters because Veo 3.1 is the same model that powers Google's paid AI video products. You're getting the same underlying technology that Google AI Pro ($19.99/month) and Ultra ($249.99/month) subscribers use, just with lower volume limits and resolution caps.

Veo 3.1 is Google DeepMind's text-to-video and image-to-video model. It generates video clips from text prompts or animates still images. The model handles natural motion, lighting, and scene composition at a level that competes with Runway Gen-4.5 and Kling 3.0 in quality benchmarks.

The access point is simple: go to vids.new in your browser. That's it. No app download, no waitlist, no credit card form.

Step-by-Step: Generate Your First Free AI Video

You can go from zero to a finished AI video clip in under 5 minutes. Here's the exact process.

Step 1: Open Google Vids

Go to vids.new in Chrome, Edge, or Firefox. Sign in with any personal Google account. If you're already logged into Gmail or Google Drive, you'll land directly on the Google Vids dashboard.

Step 2: Create a New Video Project

Click "Create new video" on the dashboard. This opens the Vids editor — a timeline-based workspace similar to Google Slides but designed for video.

Step 3: Generate an AI Clip

Click the "AI generate" button (or "Add clip" then select the AI generation option). You'll see a text prompt box. Type a description of the scene you want.

Prompt tips that work well with Veo 3.1:

  • Be specific about camera angle: "low-angle tracking shot of..." or "overhead drone view of..."
  • Include lighting details: "golden hour lighting" or "neon-lit cityscape at night"
  • Describe motion: "slowly panning right across..." or "person walking toward camera"
  • Keep it to one scene per prompt — the 8-second limit means simple is better

You can also upload a still image and have Veo 3.1 animate it — useful for product photos or illustrations you want to bring to life.

Step 4: Review and Iterate

Your clip renders in the editor. Preview it. If the result doesn't match your intent, refine your prompt and regenerate. Each generation attempt counts against your 10 monthly limit, so write detailed prompts upfront rather than burning generations on vague descriptions.

Step 5: Export or Publish

You have two options:

  • Download the clip as an MP4 file to use in other editors
  • Publish to YouTube directly from Vids (defaults to Private, so you can review before making it public)

You can also combine multiple AI-generated clips on the Vids timeline, add text overlays, and arrange them into a longer video. The timeline editor itself has no generation limit — only the AI clip creation counts against your 10.

Quality Assessment: What Can You Actually Make with 10 Free Clips?

720p resolution and 8 seconds per clip sounds limited on paper. In practice, it depends entirely on what you're making.

What Works Well

  • Social media B-roll: 8-second clips are the right length for Instagram Reels and TikTok transitions. 720p is acceptable for mobile-first platforms where most viewers watch on phones.
  • Product concept videos: Animate a product photo to show it in context — a coffee cup steaming, a shoe being worn, a gadget being unboxed. One image-to-video generation can produce a usable social ad placeholder.
  • Presentation visuals: Drop AI-generated clips into Google Slides or Keynote presentations. At 720p on a projected screen, the resolution holds up fine.
  • YouTube channel intros/outros: A branded 5-8 second clip generated once and reused across every video.
  • Mood boards and pitches: Show a client what a scene could look like before committing to production.

What Doesn't Work

  • Anything longer than 8 seconds in a single shot. You can chain clips on the timeline, but each cut will have inconsistent characters, lighting, and style — continuity across AI-generated clips is still weak across all tools, not just Veo.
  • Professional video for clients or TV. The "Made with Veo" watermark is visible and cannot be removed on the free tier. 720p also falls below broadcast standards.
  • High-volume content pipelines. 10 clips per month means you can't build a daily posting workflow around Google Vids alone. If you're posting daily, you'll burn through your allocation in two days.
  • Talking-head or avatar videos. Veo 3.1 generates cinematic footage and scenes — it doesn't create AI avatars speaking your script. For that, you need HeyGen or Synthesia.

Limitations and What You Need to Upgrade For

The free tier is generous for what it is, but there are hard walls you'll hit quickly if you're doing more than occasional clip generation.

The 10-Clip Monthly Cap

This is the biggest constraint. Ten clips sounds like enough until you realize that failed generations — clips that don't match your prompt — count against the total. In hands-on testing, expect to use 2-3 generations per "keeper" clip as you learn what prompts work best with Veo 3.1. That gives you roughly 3-5 usable clips per month.

The Watermark

Every free-tier clip carries a visible "Made with Veo" watermark. All output (including paid tiers except Ultra) also embeds SynthID, Google's invisible AI content identifier. The visible watermark rules out free-tier output for client deliverables, published ads, and most commercial use cases.

No AI Music or Avatars

Google Vids' Lyria 3 music generation and customizable AI avatars are locked behind the Pro and Ultra tiers. On the free plan, you generate silent clips. You'll need to add audio separately — royalty-free music from YouTube Audio Library or a voiceover from a tool like ElevenLabs ($5/month starter).

720p Resolution Ceiling

720p is fine for social media viewed on phones. It's noticeably soft on desktop, laptop screens, or any display larger than a tablet. If you're embedding video on a website or using it in a presentation on a large monitor, you'll see the difference.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Google Vids Veo 3.1 actually free?

Yes. As of April 2026, every personal Google account gets 10 free Veo 3.1 video generations per month through Google Vids at vids.new. No credit card, no subscription, no trial period. The free tier is ongoing, though Google notes that limits are "subject to change at any time."

Can I remove the "Made with Veo" watermark for free?

No. The visible watermark appears on all free and Pro-tier output. Only Google AI Ultra ($249.99/month) removes it, and even then, removal is subject to local regulations. All tiers embed SynthID, an invisible digital watermark that identifies content as AI-generated.

Do unused generations roll over to the next month?

Google has not confirmed whether unused free generations roll over. Based on how other Google services handle monthly quotas, the most likely behavior is that they reset at the start of each billing cycle with no carryover. Use them or lose them.

Can I use Google Vids free clips commercially?

Google's terms allow personal and limited commercial use of free-tier output, but the visible "Made with Veo" watermark makes commercial use impractical for client-facing work. For commercial AI video without watermarks, consider paid tools like HeyGen ($24/month, watermark-free exports) or Pictory ($23/month).

What's better for free AI video: Google Vids or Kling?

Different strengths. Google Vids (Veo 3.1) produces higher-quality clips but limits you to 10 per month. Kling AI gives 66 free credits daily (~6 clips per day, ~180/month) at 720p. Pick Google Vids for quality-first projects; pick Kling for volume-first workflows. Both carry watermarks on free tiers.
