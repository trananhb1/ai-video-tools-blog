InVideo vs Canva: Overview

InVideo and Canva both let you make videos online without installing anything. They target the same audience — beginners and small businesses — but they approach video from opposite directions.

InVideo is a video-first platform. Type a prompt, paste a script, or pick a template, and InVideo assembles a complete video with stock footage, voiceover, captions, music, and transitions. The v4.0 update (2026) added AI Twins for avatar cloning, voice cloning from a 30-second sample, and bundled access to frontier AI models including Sora 2 Pro, VEO 3.1, and Kling 3.0. Everything on the platform is built for video production. For the full breakdown, see our InVideo review.

Canva is a design-first platform that includes video. You can edit video in the same workspace where you create Instagram posts, pitch decks, logos, and documents. Canva added Magic Studio AI features — including text-to-video powered by Google Veo-3 — but video is one slice of a much larger toolkit. The value proposition is consolidation: one subscription, one brand kit, one login for every visual asset your business needs.

The question is not which tool is "better" in the abstract. It is whether video is your primary output or one of many things you produce. That distinction drives the right choice for most buyers.

Which Free Plan Is Actually Usable?

Both InVideo and Canva offer free plans, but they give you very different things.

InVideo Free

  • ~10 AI video minutes per week — type a prompt, get a multi-scene video with footage, voiceover, and captions
  • 4 exports per week
  • 720p resolution only
  • Watermark on every export (lower-right corner, visible throughout)
  • No commercial usage rights
  • Limited stock footage selection (basic collection, not the full 16M+ iStock library)

Canva Free

  • 5 lifetime AI video generation credits (each credit = one 4-second clip via Magic Media) — this is a demo, not a production tool
  • Unlimited manual video editing at 1080p with no watermark (using free elements)
  • 250,000+ free templates (including video templates)
  • 5 GB cloud storage
  • No brand kit, no premium stock, no background remover on video
Bottom line: If you want AI to generate your videos automatically, InVideo Free is more useful — you get weekly AI minutes versus Canva’s 5 lifetime credits. If you want to manually edit video using drag-and-drop with no watermark, Canva Free is more generous with 1080p export and unlimited editing. Know which workflow you prefer before choosing.

AI Video Features Compared

This is where InVideo and Canva diverge most. InVideo is AI-first for video; Canva sprinkles AI across a broader design suite.

InVideo AI Features

InVideo’s core product is its AI video generator. Describe what you want in plain English — "create a 90-second Instagram Reel about the benefits of cold plunging" — and the AI writes a script, pulls stock footage from the 16M+ iStock library, records a voiceover, adds captions and music, and assembles transitions. You edit by typing commands like "make the intro punchier" or "swap the third clip for something outdoors."

The v4.0 update added:

  • AI Twins: Record yourself on webcam (Express Avatar) or supply ~30 minutes of footage (Pro Avatar) to create a digital clone. Your AI Twin delivers any script in 50+ languages.
  • Voice Cloning: Upload a 30-second audio sample and the AI replicates your voice for all future videos.
  • Product Twins: Paste a product URL or upload photos and InVideo generates ad-ready video of that product in real-world contexts.
  • Frontier Models: Bundled access to Sora 2 Pro, VEO 3.1, Kling 3.0, and Seedance 2.0 for AI-generated footage within your videos.

Canva AI Features

Canva’s AI features sit inside Magic Studio and span image generation, text writing, and video. For video specifically:

  • Magic Media (Text-to-Video): Generate 4–8 second video clips from text prompts, powered by Google Veo-3 (as of May 2026, per Canva’s product page). One credit per clip. Pro users get 50 credits/month shared across all Magic Studio tools.
  • Magic Video: Upload clips and photos, type a prompt, and Canva auto-edits them with transitions and a soundtrack.
  • Highlights: AI automatically cuts long footage into short highlight clips.
  • AI Voice Generator: Text-to-speech voiceovers in multiple accents and languages.
  • Background Remover: One-click background removal on video clips (Pro only).
Bottom line: InVideo generates complete multi-scene videos from a single prompt. Canva generates short individual clips that you assemble manually. If you want "describe it and get a finished video," InVideo is the clear winner. If you want AI to assist while you build, Canva’s approach integrates into a manual editing workflow. Templates and Ease of Use

InVideo Templates

InVideo offers 5,000+ templates purpose-built for video: social media ads, YouTube intros, product demos, testimonials, real estate listings, event invitations, and more. Every template is designed specifically for video production, with pre-set timing, transitions, and scene structures. Templates are available in 16:9, 9:16, and 1:1 aspect ratios.

The editing interface has two modes: AI prompting (type natural-language commands to change your video) and a timeline editor with drag-and-drop control over individual clips, text layers, and audio tracks. The timeline gives you more granular control over pacing and structure than Canva’s editor.

Canva Templates

Canva’s 610,000+ template library is the largest in the design space, though most templates are for graphics, presentations, and print — not video. The video template subset is still substantial and covers social posts, ads, stories, and promo videos. Templates are customizable with Canva’s familiar drag-and-drop interface that works like PowerPoint: drop in text, swap images, adjust colors.

Canva’s editor is intentionally simple. There is no timeline in the traditional sense — you work with individual pages (scenes) and set durations per page. For simple social clips, this is faster than a timeline editor. For longer or more complex videos, the lack of granular timeline control becomes a limitation.

Bottom line: Canva wins on template volume and accessibility — if you have never edited a video, Canva’s interface will feel familiar within minutes. InVideo wins on video-specific depth, timeline control, and AI-assisted editing via prompt commands. Brand Kit and Collaboration

Canva dominates here. Canva Pro includes a full brand kit (logos, custom colors, fonts, brand guidelines) that applies across every design type — graphics, presentations, documents, and video. Canva Teams ($10/user/mo for 2+ people) adds real-time co-editing, shared brand kits, role-based permissions, and comment threads. For businesses that need visual consistency across all content types, Canva’s brand infrastructure is far ahead.

InVideo supports basic branding (upload logos, set brand colors) and lets you share projects for feedback, but it lacks real-time collaboration, role management, and the cross-format brand kit that Canva offers. If video is one part of your brand output and you need consistency across social posts, decks, and documents, Canva is the clear pick.

Who Should Pick Which?

Frequently Asked Questions

Is InVideo or Canva better for making videos in 2026?

It depends on your primary need. InVideo is better if video is your main output — its AI generates complete videos from a text prompt with stock footage, voiceover, captions, and transitions. Canva is better if you need video as part of a broader design workflow — social posts, presentations, brand kits, and video from one tool. For pure video creation, InVideo wins. For multi-format content, Canva wins.

Can I make videos for free on InVideo and Canva?

Yes, both offer free plans, but the limits differ significantly. InVideo Free gives approximately 10 AI video minutes per week with a watermark, 720p export, and no commercial rights. Canva Free gives only 5 lifetime AI video generation credits (one credit equals one 4-second clip) but offers unlimited manual video editing at 1080p with no watermark — as long as you use free elements. Canva’s free manual video editor is more generous; InVideo’s free AI generator gives you more to work with.

Which is cheaper, InVideo or Canva Pro?

Canva Pro is cheaper at $14.99 per month (or $119.99 per year) compared to InVideo Plus at $28 per month (or approximately $22 per month billed annually). However, Canva Pro is a general design tool with video as one feature, while InVideo Plus is entirely focused on video creation with 50 AI minutes per month, the iStock library, AI Twins, voice cloning, and access to frontier AI models like Sora 2 Pro and VEO 3.1.

Does Canva have AI video generation like InVideo?

Yes, but it is more limited. Canva uses Google Veo-3 to generate short video clips (4 to 8 seconds) from text prompts via Magic Media. Free users get 5 lifetime credits; Pro users get 50 credits per month shared across all Magic Studio AI tools. InVideo generates full multi-scene videos up to 30 minutes with script, footage, voiceover, music, and captions from a single prompt. InVideo’s AI video generation is significantly deeper.

Can I create AI avatars on InVideo and Canva?

InVideo v4.0 includes AI Twins, which let you create a digital clone of yourself from a webcam recording. It also offers an AI Actor library of pre-built avatars. Canva does not have a native AI avatar or talking-head feature as of May 2026. If you need an on-screen AI presenter, InVideo is the only option between the two.

Which tool has better templates for video?

InVideo has 5,000+ video-specific templates covering ads, intros, outros, product demos, testimonials, and social content. Canva has over 610,000 templates across all design categories, with a substantial subset dedicated to video. Canva wins on sheer template volume and variety across formats. InVideo wins on video-specific depth — every template is designed for video production, not adapted from a graphic design format.
