AI Video Subscription Checklist 2026: What to Check Before You Pay

By Tom Tran · Published September 17, 2026 · 9 min read

Quick Answer

Before paying for any AI video subscription, confirm six things: whether unused credits expire, whether failed generations are still charged, what commercial rights are included, whether your plan removes watermarks and what export resolution is allowed, what the cancellation and refund window is, and whether team or seat sharing is supported at your plan tier. All six points vary significantly across tools and change without notice.

Why This Checklist Matters

AI video tool pricing pages are optimised for conversion, not transparency. The key details that determine your real cost — what happens when a generation fails, whether you can use outputs in paid ads, whether you lose credits at month-end — are buried in Terms of Service pages, billing FAQs, or plan comparison footnotes. Founders and marketers who skip this step regularly pay for credits they can’t use, discover watermarks on outputs intended for client work, or find out a refund is unavailable after the first billing cycle has already run.

This checklist gives you the six questions to answer before entering payment details. Where possible, each point includes guidance on where to find the answer on a typical AI video tool’s site, because terms change without warning and any specific figure here could be outdated by the time you read this.

Scope note: This guide covers general SaaS subscription patterns for AI video tools as a category. It does not quote specific plan prices, which are subject to change. For tool-specific details, see the individual reviews linked throughout.

1. Credit Expiry & Rollover Policy

Most AI video platforms sell access in the form of credits (sometimes called “generations”, “render minutes”, or “video credits”). The critical question is: what happens to credits you don’t use before your billing period resets?

The two common models:

  • Reset (most common on monthly plans): Unused credits are forfeited at the end of each billing cycle. You start fresh each month. If you subscribed for a project-heavy month but then have a slow month, you lose the unused allocation.
  • Annual pooling: Some tools on annual billing let you draw from a full-year credit pool, which effectively removes the monthly expiry risk. This needs explicit confirmation — do not assume it applies.

Where to check: Look for a “Credits”, “Billing FAQ”, or “How credits work” page in the tool’s help centre. If no dedicated page exists, the Terms of Service or subscription agreement will contain the answer under “Subscription” or “Payment Terms”.

Red flag: If the pricing page does not mention rollover, assume credits do not roll over. Contact support before subscribing if you need confirmation in writing.

2. Failed-Generation Charges

AI video generation occasionally fails — a server error, a timeout, a corrupted output, or a generation the tool itself refuses to complete. Whether you are charged a credit for a failed generation depends entirely on the platform’s policy, which is rarely highlighted in marketing materials.

Common approaches:

  • Credit deducted on submission, not on success: The credit is spent the moment you click “Generate”. A failed generation is still billed. This is the approach taken by some credit-based platforms where generation jobs are queued and server costs are incurred regardless of output.
  • Credit refunded on confirmed failure: Some platforms automatically refund credits when a generation job errors out or returns an unusable result. This is more user-friendly but requires you to know how to identify and report a failure.
  • Partial deductions: A few platforms charge a fraction of a credit for failed renders.

Where to check: Search the tool’s help centre for “failed generation credit” or “generation error refund”. If nothing is documented, contact support and ask: “If a generation fails or produces an error, do I lose the credit?” — and save the response.

3. Commercial Rights & Licensing

This is the most legally consequential item on this list. Can you use AI-generated video outputs in commercial contexts — client work, paid ads, monetised YouTube channels, product listings, brand campaigns? The answer depends on (a) the tool’s Terms of Service and (b) which plan tier you are on.

Common patterns:

  • Free plans: Often restricted to personal, non-commercial use only. Outputs may not be used in paid advertising or client deliverables.
  • Entry-level paid plans: May include “commercial use” but with limits — for example, restricted to use by the account holder’s own business only, not transferable to clients.
  • Higher tiers or business plans: Typically include broad commercial rights, including client work and resale of services produced using the tool.

Some tools also restrict use of specific AI avatars (particularly licensed human likenesses) in certain categories — political advertising, adult content, and competitor advertising are common exclusions even on commercial plans.

Where to check: Read the tool’s Terms of Service section on “Intellectual Property”, “Ownership”, or “Content Rights”. The plan comparison page should also list “Commercial use: Yes/No” for each tier. If it does not, treat the licence as restricted until confirmed otherwise.

This is not legal advice. If commercial usage rights have significant financial or compliance implications for your business, consult a qualified legal professional who can review the specific tool’s terms.

4. Watermarks & Export Limits

Two separate sub-questions apply here:

4a. Watermarks

Free plans from most AI video tools add a visible watermark or platform logo to exported videos. On some tools this also applies to the lowest paid tier. Check:

  • Which plan tier removes the watermark?
  • Is the watermark only on the video export, or also burned into rendered previews you can share for approval?
  • Are there any situations where the watermark returns even on a paid plan (for example, when a content policy flag is triggered)?

4b. Export Resolution & Format Limits

Check the following on the plan comparison table or features page:

  • Maximum output resolution (720p, 1080p, 4K) — lower-tier plans often cap at 1080p or below
  • Video length per generation — some entry plans limit each clip to 30 seconds, 1 minute, or 2 minutes
  • File formats available (MP4, MOV, WebM) — some export formats are restricted to higher plans
  • Subtitle/caption file export (SRT, VTT) — relevant if you need captions for accessibility compliance or ad specs
  • API access — almost universally restricted to business or enterprise tiers; important if you intend to automate generation

Where to check: The plan comparison table on the tool’s pricing page is the primary source. Look at each row for your target use case. If something is listed as “coming soon” on a plan, do not rely on it being present when you need it.

5. Cancellation & Refund Terms

Most AI video SaaS tools operate a strict no-refund policy on monthly subscriptions once the billing period has begun. Annual subscriptions sometimes offer a short refund window (commonly 7 to 14 days) after the initial purchase. After that window, the annual fee is typically non-refundable even if you cancel immediately.

Key questions to answer before subscribing:

  • Is there a free trial with no credit card required, or does the trial require payment details?
  • If you cancel mid-month on a monthly plan, do you retain access until the end of the billing period, or is access revoked immediately?
  • Does downgrading from annual to monthly trigger any refund or credit, or does the annual fee simply stop renewing?
  • Are there any promotional discounts with early-termination clauses that claw back the discount if you cancel before a minimum term?

Where to check: The tool’s Terms of Service under “Cancellation”, “Refunds”, or “Subscription Terms”. The billing FAQ or help centre may also cover cancellation steps. If neither exists, contact support before subscribing.

Best practice: Test any tool using a free tier or free trial before upgrading to a paid annual plan. If no free option is available, use the monthly plan for at least one billing cycle before committing annually.

6. Team Seats & Seat Sharing

If you plan to use an AI video tool across a team — with multiple editors, a virtual assistant, or multiple client accounts — seat and sharing policies matter significantly. Common patterns:

  • Single-seat plans (most entry-level plans): One login, one user. Sharing credentials is often explicitly prohibited in the Terms of Service and may result in account suspension.
  • Seat add-ons: Some tools allow you to add team seats at additional cost per seat per month, without requiring an upgrade to the next plan tier.
  • Team or Business plans: Higher-tier plans often include a minimum number of seats (e.g., 3 or 5) with shared credit pools and admin dashboards.
  • Client accounts / sub-accounts: Agency-specific features — separate client workspaces, white-labelling, client-facing exports — are typically restricted to agency or enterprise plans and are worth confirming explicitly if you run an agency.

Where to check: The plan comparison table under “Users”, “Seats”, or “Collaborators”. The Terms of Service will also define whether account sharing is permitted.

Decision Rules

Use the following rules to determine whether a given tool is ready to subscribe to:

  • Proceed without hesitation: You have confirmed all six checklist items via the tool’s own documentation, a free trial is available with no card required, and the outputs from your trial meet your quality and format needs.
  • Proceed with caution: You cannot confirm one or two items from the checklist. Start on a monthly plan rather than annual until you have verified everything in practice.
  • Do not subscribe yet: Commercial rights are unclear or absent from the Terms of Service, there is no free trial and no documented refund policy for the annual plan, or credits expire on a reset schedule with no exceptions.
  • Escalate to legal review: You will be producing AI-generated content at scale for third-party clients or in regulated categories (healthcare, financial services, political advertising). The tool’s Terms of Service should be reviewed by a qualified legal professional before commercial use.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do unused AI video credits roll over between months?

It varies by tool. Most SaaS AI video platforms reset credits monthly with no rollover. Some annual plans pool credits across the year, but this must be confirmed in each tool’s own plan documentation before subscribing — terms change without notice. Check the billing or credits FAQ page on any tool’s site directly.

Can I use AI-generated videos commercially on a basic plan?

Not always. Many AI video tools restrict commercial use to paid tiers or require an upgrade above the entry-level plan. Always locate the “commercial license” or “usage rights” section in the tool’s Terms of Service or plan comparison page before producing content you intend to monetise or use in paid ads.

What happens if an AI video generation fails — am I still charged?

Policies vary widely. Some tools deduct credits on generation start regardless of whether the output is usable; others refund credits on confirmed failures. Check the tool’s support documentation for its failed-generation credit policy before you commit to a subscription.

Are refunds available after cancelling an AI video subscription?

Most AI video SaaS tools have a no-refund policy on monthly subscriptions after the billing period has started. Annual plans sometimes offer a short refund window (typically 7–14 days). Always read the cancellation policy — found in the Terms of Service or Billing FAQ — before subscribing. A free trial or free tier is the safest way to evaluate a tool before committing.

Do AI-generated videos on a free or basic plan have watermarks?

Free and low-tier plans from most AI video tools add a watermark or branding to exported videos. Whether watermarks appear on paid tiers and at what plan level they are removed depends on each tool’s plan structure. Check the “watermark” or “branding” row in the plan comparison table on the tool’s pricing page.

What resolution or export limits should I check before subscribing?

Look for maximum output resolution (1080p vs 4K), file formats available (MP4, MOV, GIF), maximum video length per generation, and whether bulk export or API access is included. Lower tiers often cap exports at 1080p or limit video length. Confirm these on the tool’s pricing or features page before you pay.
