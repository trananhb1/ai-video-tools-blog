YouTube brings Gemini conversational AI video editing to Shorts and YouTube Create

YouTube announced the integration of a new conversational editing assistant powered by Gemini into YouTube Shorts and the YouTube Create app on September 23, 2026 at its annual Made on YouTube event.[1] The update introduces prompt-driven video assembly, automated pause trimming, frame reordering, and beat-synchronized music alignment directly within the mobile creation workflow.[1]

What is documented

Official YouTube documentation confirms that creators can collaborate with Gemini as a conversational editing partner on mobile devices.[1] The tool allows users to generate initial drafts from suggested or custom prompts, or assemble clips directly from their camera rolls.[1] Creators can continue chatting with the assistant to trim awkward pauses, rearrange clips, add text hooks, and sync background music to the beat, while seamlessly jumping back and forth between the conversational AI and the manual timeline editor.[1] The base YouTube Create companion mobile app is available at zero cost on both the Australian Apple App Store and Google Play Store for eligible iOS and Android devices.[2][3] However, current storefront listings document existing editing features and camera-roll drafting, and do not yet establish that the conversational editing assistant is active on Australian accounts.[2][3]

What remains unverified

Crucially, YouTube's announcement does not confirm that the conversational assistant is already rolled out to all regions or individual Australian accounts.[unverified] Furthermore, YouTube's general Terms of Service do not explicitly grant commercial rights specifically to conversational-editor outputs or off-platform use, leaving commercial syndication rights unverified.[4] Real-world conversational turn latency, failure rates when interpreting complex revision prompts, and vocal clipping at cut boundaries remain untested.[unverified] Laboratory testing is required to verify whether commercial product details—such as microphone capsules, badges, and control knobs—survive conversational assembly without distortion.[unverified]
