## Decision

Runway Ruby is a native SDR-to-HDR conversion model, not a generative product-video model. It accepts Runway, third-party, or uploaded footage and exports HDR10, HLG, ProRes, Linear BT.2020 EXR, or ACEScg EXR. Access requires Max or Runway Dev and costs 20 credits per second; Unlimited mode does not cover it.[3]

This is relevant to advanced creator finishing workflows but does not solve AIVP's upstream exact-product generation problem. It can only preserve and grade a product already present in rights-cleared footage. The verified floor is 36/100, potential ceiling 100/100, evidence coverage 36%, confidence C.

## Why it matters

Ruby makes HDR finishing available inside Sessions, Workflows, and Runway Dev, with outputs intended for professional film/VFX pipelines.[3] Runway's current Max plan advertises studio-grade HDR, ProRes, and image-sequence generations; its published annual-billing price is US$76/month with 9,500 monthly credits.[4] At 20 credits per second, a 30-second source consumes 600 credits before any reruns or rejected outputs.

## Rights and product gate

Runway's terms define user uploads and generated outputs as “Your Content” and require the user to hold necessary rights.[5] A controlled test is still required before claiming commercial suitability for a specific source or output. Product-affiliate recommendation is blocked until Ruby demonstrates no material change to product colour, labels, controls, skin tone, or highlight detail across three bounded runs.
