Runway Ruby First Look: Native SDR-to-HDR, With a High Entry Cost

Runway has documented a specialist HDR finishing model for existing footage. The workflow is credible on paper, but exact-product colour fidelity and practical cost remain untested.

What Ruby does

Ruby converts existing SDR video—including uploaded camera footage and third-party generations—into HDR formats. It runs in Sessions, Workflows and Runway Dev, and costs 20 credits per source second.[3]

Who it is for

The supported ProRes, ACEScg, EXR, HDR10 and HLG formats point toward professional finishing rather than beginner social-video creation.[3] Ruby requires Max or Runway Dev, so it is not included with the lower creator plans.[3][4]

AIVP product-video verdict

Conditional only. Ruby does not create a specific product; it grades footage that already contains one. A recommendation requires three controlled runs showing that product colour, labels, controls and highlight detail survive conversion.

Rights and cost caution

Users remain responsible for rights in uploaded and generated content.[5] A 30-second conversion consumes 600 credits at the documented rate, before reruns; cost per accepted output is unknown until tested.
