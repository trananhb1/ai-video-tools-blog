## Primary findings

Fotor positions Video Agent for explainers, presenter videos, product ads, launch videos, montages and editable motion-graphic effects.[2]

Its product-video page says the workflow accepts product photos, briefs and links from Amazon, Shopify, eBay and Shopee, then builds scripts, scenes, transitions, captions and audio.[3]

That is unusually aligned with AIVP's exact-product affiliate workflow, but AIVP has not verified whether the linked product remains recognizable in the first 10 seconds or whether listing details are reproduced accurately.

The general pricing page advertises a free Basic tier with limited credits but does not establish Video Agent run/export limits.[6]

Separate credit documentation lists generation costs for short AI video clips, but does not identify the cost of a complete Video Agent project.[7]

## Rights limitation

Fotor's terms say users remain responsible for generated output and that Fotor currently makes no copyright claim over AIGC output, while also saying Fotor cannot license or release that output to the user.[4]

The commercial-use help article clearly addresses AI-generated images and Fotor materials, but does not clearly grant monetized commercial rights for complete Video Agent outputs containing video, audio, templates or third-party assets.[5]

Commercial publication is therefore blocked pending written confirmation covering the complete exported video and included audio/media.
