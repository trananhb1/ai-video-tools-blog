Inkitt launches Movie Creator public beta for AI script-to-movie generation

Digital publisher Inkitt launched Inkitt Movie Creator in public beta on September 24, 2026, offering a browser-based platform designed to turn text-based scripts into AI-generated films in minutes.[1][2] The public beta page is publicly reachable through Inkitt's web portal at https://www.inkitt.com/video/script-to-video, while AU-specific signup, render, and payment access remains unverified.[1][2][unverified]

What is documented

Official product documentation on the Inkitt portal presents a 3-step creation workflow: creators upload their script, generate bespoke characters, props, and scenes, and build trailers, shows, and movies.[2] The user interface features granular scene direction controls allowing storytellers to adjust Tone (e.g., Wistful), Pace (e.g., Slow burn), Light (e.g., Golden), Score (e.g., Sparse), and Look (Cinematic, Illustrated, Noir, Painterly), with multi-take rendering capabilities across scenes.[2] Inkitt prominently claims that 100% of the rights and creative decisions remain with the storyteller, declaring that zero agents or greenlights stand between creators and their film premieres.[2] Official metadata notes the web tool is free to try.[2]

Architecture and VentureBeat reporting

According to reporting by VentureBeat, Inkitt Movie Creator is powered by Cinematica, an in-house orchestration harness that coordinates diverse language, image, and video foundation models (including ByteDance Seedance and MiniMax) rather than training a standalone video foundation model.[1] Cinematica encodes professional filmmaking logic, managing shot breakdowns, scene pacing, and character continuity.[1] VentureBeat reports that Inkitt estimates generation and AI-production costs at approximately $75 to $500 per finished minute, depending heavily on the number of generation takes per scene and underlying model selection.[1] Crucially, VentureBeat reports there is no public API available at launch, and while Inkitt operates a Model Context Protocol (MCP) server, it is not yet connected to the Cinematica production tool.[1]
