Synthesia Dubbing 2.0 for External Videos: First Look

Synthesia announced Dubbing 2.0 on July 15, 2026, so a September 23 news listing is late secondary coverage rather than a same-day launch.[1][5]

Official documentation says users can upload external MP4, WebM, or MOV footage or a public YouTube link, preserve the original speaker voice, add lip sync, and use bulk or API workflows.[2] The public translator offers a first free minute and requires uploaders to hold the necessary source rights.[3]

What is credible now

  • External-video and YouTube inputs are documented.[2]
  • Voice preservation, optional lip sync, transcript controls, glossary support, and bulk dubbing are documented.[1][2]
  • A no-cost evaluation route is advertised, but account allowances and lip-sync credit use must be checked live.[2][3]

What remains unknown

AIVP has not verified output quality, consistency, speed, terminology accuracy, watermark behavior, accepted-output cost, or complete monetized-output rights. This is not a Review and carries no ranking recommendation.

Planned test

Three identical localizations of one rights-cleared 30-second AIVP talking-head explainer, with fixed glossary, target language, lip-sync and duration settings. Generation stops before any charge or paid-credit use.
