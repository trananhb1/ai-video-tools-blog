LTX-2.5 W4A8 ConvRot: First Look at a 24GB-GPU Quantization

JoaoZaokk's new checkpoint re-encodes LTX-2.5's distilled 22B transformer into ComfyUI W4A8. It is a deployment derivative, not a new foundation model.[1][4]

What is documented

The 12.52GB transformer was tested by its publisher on one 249-frame, 512×512 render using an RTX 3090. The reported run took 400.9 seconds and used 11.66 GiB for the transformer, versus 780.7 seconds and 39.13 GiB for BF16. It was less faithful to BF16 than Lightricks' INT8 checkpoint: 25.39 dB versus 29.71 dB PSNR.[1]

The checkpoint itself is still a 12.5GB download, requires other LTX components, and its large-file security scans were not complete when assessed.[2]

Why it matters—and what is missing

Fitting the transformer on a 24GB consumer card is meaningful for specialist local workflows. But the benchmark covers one prompt, seed, resolution and GPU; it did not decode audio, test vertical output, measure exact-product identity or report repeat failures.[1]

Rights

LTX's current community licence describes no-cost production/commercial use below a US$10 million entity-wide annual-revenue threshold and says Lightricks claims no rights in generated outputs, subject to the full licence and acceptable-use restrictions.[3][4]

Early verdict

Weak fit, confidence C, verified floor 44/100. The benchmark is more useful than an unmeasured upload, but this remains a specialist component rather than a ready creator workflow. AIVP cannot recommend it for product affiliate video until three controlled LED-mask runs preserve exact product identity in the first 10 seconds.
