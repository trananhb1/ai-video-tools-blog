LTX-2.3 W4A8 ConvRot: First Look at a Measured Audio-Video Quantization

JoaoZaokk's new checkpoint re-encodes the LTX-2.3 distilled 1.1 transformer to W4A8 while retaining the audio-video components in one file. It is a deployment derivative, not a new foundation model.[1][4]

What is documented

The 15.51 GiB checkpoint includes publisher-supplied MP4 and FLAC artifacts. In one 249-frame, 512×512, eight-step RTX 3090 run, W4A8 sampled in 18 seconds and measured 10.39 MAE and 21.89 dB PSNR against BF16. A 6-bit Q6_K control was closer at 3.59 MAE and 29.00 dB but sampled in 40 seconds.[1]

W4A8 and Q6_K both measured 0.163 log-mel L1 against BF16 in the publisher's audio comparison. Those metrics are distances, not human quality ratings, and the test covers only one prompt, seed, resolution and card.[1]

Why it matters—and what is missing

This is more evidence than a typical community quant upload: the repository includes synchronized outputs, a saved-conditioning identity control, negative results and LoRA output tests. It still does not establish vertical output, three-run reliability or exact-product identity preservation.[1][2]

The large checkpoint's Hugging Face security scans were queued or unscanned when assessed, and no inference provider serves it.[1][2]

Rights

The LTX-2 Community License grants use subject to restrictions, requires entities at or above US$10 million annual revenue to obtain a paid commercial licence, says Lightricks claims no rights in generated output and requires machine-generated disclosure.[3]

Early verdict
