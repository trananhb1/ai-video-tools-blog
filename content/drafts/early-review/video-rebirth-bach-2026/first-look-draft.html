Video Rebirth BACH First Look: Multi-Shot AI Video Hits #3 on Independent Leaderboard

Published: [date TBD] · 4 min read · First Look — docs only

What is Video Rebirth BACH?

BACH is an AI video engine from Video Rebirth, a Singapore-based startup founded by Dr. Wei Liu, a former Tencent Distinguished Scientist and IEEE/AAAS Fellow.[1][2] The company raised $80 million in AMD-backed funding.[2]

The product lives at bach.art and offers text-to-video, image-to-video, element-to-video, and a distinctive "Montage" feature that generates multi-shot films up to 30 seconds long from a single reference-plus-prompt workflow.[1][2]

Why it surfaced today

On September 16, 2026, Video Rebirth announced via LinkedIn that their BACH 1.0 Pro model has advanced to #3 on the Artificial Analysis Image-to-Video leaderboard — up from #6 on debut in April 2026 and #5 earlier this year.[3][4] The advancement is attributed to a post-training process called Recursive Self-Improvement, where the model generates videos, evaluates them against an internal quality standard, and retrains on successful outputs.[3]

BACH 1.0 Pro is expected to go live at bach.art by end of September 2026.[3]

Leaderboard context

The Artificial Analysis Video Arena uses blind pairwise human preference votes translated to Elo scores — the same methodology as competitive chess ratings. At the time of this report, the Image-to-Video leaderboard shows:

  • #1 Google Gemini Omni Flash
  • #2 MiniMax H3
  • #3 BACH 1.0 Pro (Video Rebirth) [announced, not yet live][3]

For context, Bach-1.0 Preview (current live product) showed an Elo of approximately 1,215 on Text-to-Video rankings at crafiq.ai, comparing favourably to Runway Gen-4.5 (#27, Elo ~1,214) and PixVerse V5.5 (#30, Elo ~1,194).[4]

What makes BACH different

Most AI video generators produce single clips. BACH's "Montage" feature generates up to 30-second multi-shot films — multiple camera angles, cuts, and transitions — from a single workflow, with the same character held consistently across every shot.[1][2]

Two proprietary architectures underpin this: Physics-Native Attention (PNA) for character consistency and physical realism, and Dual Diffusion Transformer (DDiT) for directorial control (whip pans, rack focus, Rembrandt lighting setups).[1][2]

The API also exposes an "Element to Video" endpoint designed for placing a specific subject (product, character) into a described scene — which maps directly to AIVP's product-specific affiliate clip use case.[1]

Pricing and access

Free sign-up credits are available on sign-up at bach.art.[2] The pricing page was returning a 404 at the time of this report; specific per-credit or per-plan costs are not yet confirmed. A REST API is documented at bach.art/docs with text2video, image2video, montage2video, element2video, and text2image endpoints.[1]
