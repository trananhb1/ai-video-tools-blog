COEY GroundSlate First Look: A Local MCP Video Editor for Mac

Draft status: internal, noindex, not approved for publication.

COEY announced GroundSlate on September 9, 2026 as a Mac-native editor driven through MCP rather than a traditional timeline.[1] The official product page describes local media indexing, search, AI generation, vertical reframing, graphics, editing and final rendering through an agent-callable toolset.[2]

What is materially new?

The differentiator is the architecture: a connected assistant can call GroundSlate's local tools to inspect a media library and produce finished video files. COEY says the app can also generate video locally with LTX 2.5, starting from text or a still image.[2] These are vendor-documented capabilities, not AIVP test findings.

Price and requirements

The free licence covers one Mac, one profile and 2,500 catalog items, but exported renders carry a visible COEY mark. Pro is listed at US$99 per year and removes the mark and catalog/profile limits.[3][5] Local AI Video has a demanding hardware floor: macOS 26, an M3-or-newer Max/Ultra chip and 64 GB unified memory.[2]

Commercial-use and privacy caveats

COEY's master terms allow lawful commercial use of outputs, subject to third-party terms.[4] The GroundSlate schedule says LTX 2.5 is separately licensed and that users must satisfy its eligibility conditions, including any applicable revenue ceiling.[5] Processing is described as local, but information returned to a connected cloud assistant may be transmitted to that assistant's provider.[5][6]

Who should watch it?

GroundSlate may matter to Apple-silicon creators with large owned-footage libraries who want agent-directed editing and local processing. It is not yet an AIVP recommendation. Product fidelity, output quality, consistency, render reliability and real cost per accepted video remain untested.
