## Documented capability and limits

The live product page says a user can request rough cuts, supercuts and vertical reels from uploaded footage, refine the result conversationally, open and change the timeline, restore every turn, choose Fast/Balanced/Max edit modes and reframe to four listed aspect ratios.[1] The same page lists Plus at US$20/month with 2,000 AI credits and 10 projects, Pro at US$40/month with 4,000 credits and 30 projects, and Studio at US$100/month with 10,000 credits and unlimited projects; all list a three-day trial.[1]

Those are vendor-documented capabilities, not AIVP results. The page does not define credit consumption by operation, measured failure rates, export codec details, project interchange, API availability, Australia-specific billing, or whether a payment method is required for the trial.[1] The older YC post says editing and clipping APIs were planned, so API access is not counted as currently available.[2]

## Commercial rights, privacy and product-specificity boundary

Narrative's terms say uploaded material stays with the user and grant Narrative a service-only licence to store, copy, transcode, analyse, display and share it with listed processors to deliver the edit.[4] That is useful input-ownership language, but the retrieved terms do not explicitly grant commercial/monetized output rights; publication remains blocked pending written confirmation.

Because Narrative edits user-supplied footage rather than fabricating a product from text, it has a plausible exact-product advantage: rights-cleared footage can show the actual ASIN. This is not verified until all three runs keep the exact product distinctive in the first 10 seconds and do not substitute generated B-roll. No generated edit may be represented as physical product-performance proof.

The privacy page confirms that Narrative collects usage/device/location/error data, while the terms confirm footage processing.[4][5] Only rights-cleared, non-sensitive fixture footage should be uploaded during testing.
